# Guard staffing and shift audit, 26 October 2026

The native location type, actual resident census and surveyed gate geometry determine the roster. Household count is not resident count. This change supplies distinct day and night personnel; it does not duplicate scene actors or multiply a saved target repeatedly.

## Allocation before and after

Let `R` be the number of residents, `G` the number of surveyed gates, `W` the number of verified reachable wall posts, and `B` the previous ordinary guard quota. The ordinary quota is capped at 700. The new complete roster is `2 * (max(3, B) + 4 * G + W)`. Each shift has `max(3, B) + 4 * G + W` identities before deaths or unavailable staff. Wall sites use approximately 20 m spacing on actual reachable surfaces, up to 128 per settlement. Two extra identities per verified wall post preserve ordinary town and gate coverage. Dawn and dusk are the native constants, 06:00 and 18:00.

| Native `DFRegion.LocationTypes` | Policy scale | Previous complete roster | New complete roster | Ordinary staff per shift |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `TownCity` (0) | City | `B + 4G`, `B = min(700, 2 * max(16, ceil(.08R)))` | `2B + 8G + 2W` | `B`, minimum 32 |
| `TownHamlet` (1) | Town | `B + 4G`, `B = min(700, 2 * max(8, ceil(.08R)))` | `2B + 8G + 2W` | `B`, minimum 16 |
| `TownVillage` (2), `R < 40` | Hamlet | `2 + 4G` | `6 + 8G + 2W` | 3 |
| `TownVillage` (2), `R >= 40` | Hamlet | `B + 4G`, `B = min(700, 2 * max(8, ceil(.08R)))` | `2B + 8G + 2W` | `B`, minimum 16 |
| `HomeFarms` (3), `ReligionTemple` (5), `Tavern` (6), `HomeWealthy` (8) | Hamlet guard scale, separate from household population admission | 0 managed municipal guards | `6 + 8G + 2W` with no resident census; actual census otherwise follows the Hamlet formula | 3 minimum |
| `DungeonLabyrinth` (4), `DungeonKeep` (7), `ReligionCult` (9), `DungeonRuin` (10), `HomePoor` (11), `Graveyard` (12), `Coven` (13), `HomeYourShips` (14), `None` (65535) | Unsupported by municipal guards | 0 managed municipal guards | 0 managed municipal guards | 0 |

Unsupported locations can still contain native or quest-owned guards. A temple or tavern *building inside a supported town* does not change the town's location type or create a separate municipal roster.

Examples without gates or accessible walls: a 12-resident village changes from 2 to 6 guards (3 day, 3 night); a 30-resident native hamlet from 16 to 32 (16 per shift); an 80-resident city from 32 to 64 (32 per shift); a 1,000-resident city from 160 to 320 (160 per shift). The native Daggerfall fixture confirms 290 residents and four gates: its previous 64 becomes a base 128 plus two personnel per verified wall post. For example, 64 accessible wall posts would produce 256 total; this is an example, not the measured final wall count.

## Duties and existing behavior

Previously, most guards remained at posts continuously. Up to two pairs patrolled at night only. Those four patrol guards could seek beds during daytime. Each open gate had two inside and two outside guards; outside guards returned before dusk and could use real beds or an arbitrary interior walking route. The latter route was not a wall-top archer assignment.

Now, both shifts use the existing surveyed patrol system. About one third of ordinary personnel patrol in pairs, capped by `MaximumPatrolPairs`, now configurable from 0 to 16 with default 8. A tiny village uses one pair plus one post. At least one ordinary available guard remains at a fixed town post; gate personnel are additional. Missing safe circuits leave guards posted. Explicit existing caps remain respected; the installed profile had no `Guards` override when inspected. `DayPatrols` and `NightPatrols` control their respective shifts. Existing night torches remain active, with the existing light budget.

For the 290-resident/four-gate Daggerfall fixture, the intended settled normal duty split per shift is 16 gate guards, 16 patrollers (eight pairs), 32 fixed town posts and `W` wall archers. Another `64 + W` identities are off duty, seeking or occupying real beds where available. The earlier four-archer design was verified for daytime and then superseded by the user's density requirement. Final density and both normal shifts remain subject to expanded native acceptance.

While Daggerfall's native global variable 0 (`LiftedCurse`) is false, the night shift instead orders every exterior guard into actual wall quarters, including wall archers. Shelter preparation starts at 18:00, before native quest S0000977 starts its 19:00–05:00 danger window; normal exterior guard duty remains suspended until 06:00 or the curse is lifted. Existing beds are preferred; occupied or bedless quarters can supply surveyed clear indoor standing points. No outdoor wall post is treated as shelter. Normal night coverage and unresolved haunting are separate controlled native fixture cases.

The later wall-trespass request adds a local attack rule from 18:00 inclusive to 06:00 exclusive. An arrived wall guard considers any living entity, including the player and otherwise passive NPCs. Managed guards and native CityWatch are exempt. The target must stand above an actual wall-block floor, at least 3 m above the street, on an upper NavMesh surface connected to a surveyed wall access. The observer uses its native sight radius, sight modifier and field of view, then physical line of sight before existing native arrow creation and damage handling. The haunting shelter order suppresses this rule. The native fixture checks player and non-player arrows, a street-level target at the same horizontal position, legitimate guards and a solid sight blocker. This native acceptance remains pending.

The roster persists each guard's shift. Replacements inherit the deceased guard's shift. Emergencies finish before ordinary shift/rest behavior resumes. Off-duty gate guards use a surveyed route back to their distinct relief post when no bed journey already takes them away. A guard without a reachable unreserved bed remains visibly off duty and available for emergencies; the system does not invent quarters or beds.

The unresolved-haunting order also interrupts exterior response, search, return and engagement states. `TakeRefuge` admits those extra states only for `HauntingShelter`, requires the real current position and a separately surveyed route back to the assigned post, and clears the active incident only after accepting the actual shelter plan. Exterior combat is suspended while that order is active. The ordinary shift policy retains its emergency precedence. The controlled haunted fixture includes an existing town guard set to an active engagement before dusk. Core first rejected this transition, then the full isolated suite passed 2185/2185, including responding, searching, returning and engaged variants; runtime compilation passes. Actual haunted native acceptance is still pending.

Outside gate guards retain the existing recall deadline: native dusk minus the actual surveyed return travel time minus 60 game seconds. The outgoing guard remains assigned until the return is complete, then walks to its relief post before the ordinary bed planner takes over. The older direct gate-to-bed shortcut was removed because it reserved inner staging throughout the night and blocked relief. Normal night relief targets inside staging only; haunting suspends those stations. Morning exterior assignments require the native gate to be open and sufficient time for the outward journey plus the pre-close return. Patrol circuits and wall-access streets reject routes crossing a gate, so cached night circuits cannot pass through a closed aperture.

## Real beds and wall support

The installed `Detailed City Walls` package is version 1.2.3. Its bundled readme describes guard barracks, storehouses, armories, training rooms and a jail; exterior walkways gain torches and gates gain ballistas. Package presence does not prove every wall quarter contains a reachable bed or every upper walkway has a walking connection.

Production rest surveys use `RefugeGeometry` and `BedGeometry.Read`, including current replacement-layout records. They do not call the old `WallQuarterBeds.Furnish` helper or invent furniture coordinates. A bed is reserved by building and native record, with an exterior route and a surveyed bedside route. Departure from the bed accounts for the full return duration, so the guard can reach its post at the next shift start.

The native CityWatch uses texture archive 399, and the existing sleep catalog already contains the matching 399/15 appearance. `SleepingPose.BeginGuard` reuses that atlas, aligns the pose to the actual mattress, hides the standing sprite, and restores the native controller and sprite before the physical return. Unknown replacement art has no fabricated fallback face.

No wall-top archer assignment existed before this change. `GuardArchery` previously supplied a bow to a deterministic 20% of ordinary guards; that was not a wall role. The removed arbitrary interior patrol fallback must not be described as a wall-top patrol.

The first native audit found 134 quarter doors, 28 wall blocks, 1,016 readable upward-facing upper triangles, 688 distinct sample points, and 554 NavMesh-supported upper surfaces. No direct street-to-upper-surface path existed. This does **not** establish inaccessibility: the user's correction identifies the native lower-door/interior-stairs/upper-door route. That route is now explicitly modeled and needs its own native acceptance.

`GuardWallWatch` pairs lower and upper exterior doors by owner, building key, native block, building record and distinct door index. It surveys the actual interior from the lower entrance to the upper exit, then uses complete upper NavMesh paths to real wall surface triangles. Multiple posts can share one stair access; the shared upper doorway is transit, not their reserved destination. `GuardRefugeJourney` preserves the two door identities and three-dimensional wall paths; reverse phases use the same walkway, stairs and street route. Every wall-role guard receives and equips a native bow. Existing native arrows, target evidence and line-of-sight checks handle visible hostile targets while the guard holds the wall post. Native CityWatch art has no ranged attack frames; this change does not fabricate a bow animation.
